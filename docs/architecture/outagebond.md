# OutageBond architecture

OutageBond converts a covered historical service outage into an onchain compensation result. A provider registers the service, canonical URL, registered operator status-page URL, region, named beneficiary, minimum duration, source tolerance, per-incident payout, maximum incident slots, and coverage period. All maximum slot payouts are escrowed at creation. There is no administrator, protocol fee, or post-creation policy edit.

The beneficiary may submit a completed incident of at most 24 hours during the covered interval and seven-day claim grace period. The claim pins its interval and both evidence URLs, and reserves every UTC day it touches (including both days across midnight). The operator URL must use the committed status-page host; the monitor must use a different host from both the operator and the service. Distinct hosts do not prove independent ownership. Each claim reserves one payout from available coverage collateral.

`attest(claim_id)` is permissionless after the incident ends. Each validator independently fetches both public HTTPS pages (bounded to 12 KB), extracts only service/region match, outage status, timestamps, and confidence, and deterministically derives a per-source verdict. Consensus requires the same final outcome and the same per-source classifications, with extracted facts and intervals meeting the contract's agreement policy. The source model does not provide persisted quotations or user-facing explanations; the contract stores facts and deterministic protocol reasons only.

The minimum-duration boundary is part of the verdict, not merely a numeric tolerance check. For example, 298s and 302s source readings within a 60s timestamp tolerance still disagree against a 300s minimum. This yields `UNVERIFIABLE`, never an eligible payout. Each extracted outage must be wholly inside the coverage period, complete at observation time, and touch exactly the submitted UTC days. Timestamp tolerance cannot move an incident across a coverage, duration, or calendar-day boundary. Validators independently reconstruct every stored source classification, committed URL, derived outcome, and deterministic explanation; altered leader metadata is rejected.

An unverifiable claim stays pending for up to three committed attempts separated by at least 60 seconds. A separate, immutable deadline is 24 hours after submission. At or after that deadline, anyone can call `expire_claim` to release collateral even if every consensus attempt failed and rolled back its counter. Expiry marks it `UNVERIFIABLE` without inferring an outage. Three committed inconclusive attempts or one ineligible decision also release the reservation. Eligible payouts remain claimable by the beneficiary for 30 days, after which anyone may release them. Collection decrements eligible capacity and increments paid capacity exactly once. The provider can recover unused collateral after coverage expiry, seven-day claim grace, and resolution of reservations; there is no automatic timer transaction.

The frontend persists intent, original reference, exact terms, and transaction hash before/between wallet and consensus steps. Web Locks serialize writes across tabs. Unknown outcomes remain locked across reloads until reconciled with the original transaction and finalized record. Finality alone is not success. Explicit wallet rejection or proven terminal failure can safely unlock; a connection timeout cannot. If no hash returns, creation and submission can be reconciled by immutable reference/day plus exact terms. Other hashless writes intentionally remain blocked for manual wallet inspection rather than being blindly repeated. Clearing browser storage removes this client-side safeguard, so users must not clear it to retry an uncertain transaction.

Direct tests cover authorization, both sides of midnight, coverage boundaries, threshold-straddling readings, failed-consensus rollback and expiry, independent validator forgery rejection, malformed extraction, payout capacity, retry timing, and collateral release. Browser-state unit tests cover ambiguous confirmations, reloads, duplicate prevention, receipt validation, and storage failure. The dedicated CI workflow runs contract lint/tests plus frontend tests/typecheck/build. The deploy script verifies finalized execution, exact source hash, and immutable configuration before recording and activating an address. The resume-safe acceptance runner verifies real StudioNet consensus and native credited transfers using dedicated test wallets.

All values on StudioNet are test GEN with no monetary value. This is an experimental demo, not a production insurance product.
